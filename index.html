How it works

Three steps. You check the recipe before anything is rewritten.

Step 1. 1 Snap

Photograph a printed recipe — a cookbook page, a magazine clipping, a card, or a printout. DishLift reads the title, ingredients, and steps. You correct anything it got wrong before it changes the dish.

Step 2. 2 Choose your goals

Pick one or more goals — more protein, less sodium, less sugar, or all three — in a single makeover. DishLift reworks the recipe that way and puts the original beside the new version, with a plain reason for each swap.

Step 3. 3 Cook

Save the version you want in your cookbook and make it. An AI picture of the dish is labeled as generated, so you know it is a picture of the idea, not a photo of your plate.

Pick one goal, or all three

Choose one or more goals for a single makeover. The dish should still be recognizable. DishLift says what stayed and what changed.

More protein

Swaps that add protein and still taste like the dish you started with. Whole foods come first. It should not turn into a different meal.

Less sodium

Less salt, and fewer salty packaged ingredients. Flavor is rebuilt with herbs, acid, spices, aromatics, and better pantry choices.

Less sugar

For desserts and other sweet recipes, a lighter hand with sugar, with texture and browning kept in mind, so it still tastes like dessert.

In the app

DishLift starts from your recipe, then keeps the version you want.

You confirm the read

The photo becomes a recipe you can edit: title, servings, ingredients, and steps. Nothing is transformed until you say the read is right.

Original and reworked, side by side

Changed ingredients and steps are called out, with a reason for each swap, so you can accept it, tweak it, or leave the original alone.

A picture of the dish

DishLift can make an image from the recipe’s name, ingredients, and method. The image is labeled AI-generated. It is not a photo of a dish you cooked, and it is not a copy of the page you photographed.

Chapters in your cookbook

Original and reworked recipes live in a private cookbook library, organized in chapters. The library stays private on your device.

A saved recipe can show estimated calories, protein, sodium, and sugar per serving. The numbers are estimates, and the app labels them that way.

Not medical or dietary advice

DishLift’s suggestions are general cooking ideas. They are not medical or dietary advice, and they are not a diagnosis, a treatment, or a meal plan for a health condition. Nutrition figures are estimates from the recipe as read, not values from a lab or a package label.

A generated picture is labeled as AI-made. If you are under a clinician’s care, or you are cooking around an allergy, check with a qualified professional and read the ingredient labels yourself. A photo of a recipe cannot prove that a dish is allergen-free.
