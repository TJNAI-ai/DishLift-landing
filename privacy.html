Privacy policy

Last updated October 20, 2026

This notice covers the website at dishlift.app and the DishLift iOS app from TJNAI.ai LLC. DishLift is not on the App Store yet. The app sections describe the product as it is designed. If the shipping app handles data differently, this page will be updated before release.

Who we are

DishLift is made by TJNAI.ai LLC, a North Carolina limited liability company. Email info@tjnai.ai. Our studio site is tjnai.ai.

This website

The dishlift.app website uses no cookies and no tracking. It does not set cookies, does not use browser storage, does not run analytics, and does not load third-party scripts. The pages are static files. There is no account and no form on this site.

If you email info@tjnai.ai, we receive whatever you put in that message — your address, and anything you write — and we use it to reply and to keep a record of the conversation. Please don’t send recipe photos or health details by email unless you mean to.

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may keep ordinary server logs for hosting, such as an IP address, browser type, and the time of a request. We do not add our own logging or analytics on top of that. GitHub’s privacy statement is at docs.github.com.

The app, in short

DishLift is a cooking app. You photograph a recipe. The app reads it, can suggest a version with more protein, less sodium, or less sugar, can make an AI image of the dish, and can keep the result in a private cookbook.

Suggestions are general cooking ideas. They are not medical or dietary advice. Goals and food preferences you choose to save are personal. We treat them as private.

Recipe photos

When you photograph or choose a recipe image, the app sends that photo to a Supabase backend. A Supabase edge function calls Anthropic’s Claude to turn the photo into a structured recipe: fields such as title, servings, ingredients, and steps. In the current backend notes the default model is Claude Sonnet 4.6 (claude-sonnet-4-6). The model name can change.

A recipe photo can hold more than a recipe. A page may include names, handwriting, an address, a church or community name, or other notes. Treat the photo as personal information.

You are asked to review and correct the extracted recipe before a transformation is applied. The design keeps the original photo for your reference only if you allow that storage, and it lets you delete the photo while keeping the structured recipe.

Rewriting a recipe

Reading the photo and rewriting the recipe are different steps. The materials behind this notice document Claude for reading the photo. They do not yet name the model that writes the new version for the goal you pick. This page will name that provider, and say whether the request includes dietary preferences or other choices you saved, once that path is fixed for release.

AI images of a dish

After you confirm a recipe, the app can create a picture of what the finished dish could look like. The picture is built from the recipe — name, ingredients, and method — and it is labeled AI-generated. It is not a photo of a dish you cooked, and the design says it must not copy the page you photographed. The design stores the picture with the recipe, along with the prompt and style used to make it.

On your device and on our servers

A trial without an account is part of the design, so a first use does not require you to register. What follows is the split the design describes. The shipping build should be checked against it before release.

  • Sent to the server: the recipe photo, so Supabase can pass it to Claude for extraction. Recipes, generated images, and preferences are designed to be savable in the library, which means a server copy when that library is synced.
  • Kept on the device in the current design: a count of complimentary makeovers, so the app knows when a purchase would be required. The plan is to move that count to an account or an Apple CloudKit record before public launch. It is not a device fingerprint.
  • Only if you allow it: keeping the original photo, and saving health-related preferences.

Preferences the design can store, when you choose to set them, include goals, dietary preferences, allergens, ingredients you would rather avoid, and sweetener preference. You do not have to save these.

Nutrition estimates

The app can show estimated calories, protein, sodium, and sugar per serving, with a sign when the estimate is uncertain. The design uses USDA FoodData Central as the baseline food-composition reference. These are estimates from the recipe as read, not laboratory or package-label values.

Purchases

No one can pay for DishLift today. When a purchase is offered, Apple In-App Purchase is the payment system. RevenueCat is the planned way the app checks a “pro” entitlement and restores a purchase. Purchase records are not supposed to include your recipes or food preferences.

How we use information

  • To read a recipe photo and show you the structured recipe.
  • To rewrite a recipe for a goal you chose, and to explain the swaps.
  • To make and store an AI image of the dish, labeled as generated.
  • To keep the private cookbook you save.
  • To remember preferences you opt in to save.
  • To tell the app whether a purchase is active, once purchases exist.
  • To answer email you send us.

We do not sell personal information. We do not use your private recipe photos to train models unless you explicitly opt in.

Who else processes it

To run the app as designed, data is handled by:

  • Supabase — backend for the extract function and the library storage the design calls for.
  • Anthropic — Claude, to read the recipe photo.
  • An image provider — not yet named in this notice. See the review note in the page source. The picture is part of the product; the vendor will be listed here before release.
  • Apple — the App Store and In-App Purchase, when those exist, and CloudKit if the makeover count moves there.
  • RevenueCat — entitlement checks, when purchases exist.
  • GitHub — hosting for this website only.

Where a company stores a given request depends on that service and on our project settings. This notice will name the Supabase region before release.

How long we keep it

Email is kept as long as we need it to handle the thread, and then deleted when we no longer need it. Recipe photos, recipes, images, and preferences are kept so the app can show you your cookbook, until you delete them or ask us to.

Deletion and export

The product is designed so you can delete a recipe, delete an uploaded photo while keeping the structured recipe, and delete saved preferences. It is also designed so you can export your data. For a copy or a deletion we have to do on the server, email info@tjnai.ai. We will respond within a reasonable time.

Security

Recipe data is sent over HTTPS. The product is designed to encrypt recipe photos and generated images at rest. No method of storage is perfect. Please don’t photograph a page you would not want a cooking service to read.

Children

DishLift is a general cooking app for adults. It is not directed at children under 13, and it is not for pediatric diets. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has sent a photo or other personal information, email info@tjnai.ai and we will delete it.

Changes

If we change this notice, we will change the date at the top. For the app, a material change will be in the build you download, not only on this page.
